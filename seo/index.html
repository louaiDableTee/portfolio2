Found · Technical and on-page SEO

Google could not read the site.

The skill

Make a site crawlable, then make each page answer one search intent.

The problem

A manual Googlebot fetch returned 403 through Cloudflare. robots.txt and the sitemap both returned 404. No structured data. Old applicant-tracking routes were still known to Google.

The proof

A sitemap accepted with 14 pages, 13 old routes redirected, and JSON-LD validating with zero errors.

Order of operations

Foundations first, words second.

There is no point optimising a title tag on a page Google cannot fetch.

  1. Restored crawl access. Diagnosed the 403 returned to Googlebot and got the block on crawling removed.
  2. Corrected robots.txt. It was returning 404. It now exists and is structured.
  3. Submitted a sitemap. Accepted by Search Console with 14 pages found.
  4. Handled 13 old routes. Legacy applicant-tracking URLs now redirect instead of dead-ending.
  5. Rewrote titles and H1s. One search intent per page, no page competing with another.
  6. Strengthened internal linking. Connected the homepage to the recruiter and sales pages deliberately.

Method

How I chose the words.

I start from what a page is for, who lands on it and what they need. Then I cross six sources.

  1. Real product features. What the tool actually does, in the words the product uses.
  2. Customer vocabulary. How recruiters and sales teams describe the problem themselves.
  3. Search Console queries. What people already reach the site with.
  4. Manual analysis of Google results. Reading the actual page one for each target query.
  5. Existing site content. What the site already ranks for and already says.
  6. Competitors. How comparable sourcing tools frame the same need.

A deliberate choice

Qualitative, not volume-driven

  • I used no unverified search volumes. For a young B2B product in a small market, volume estimates are thin and often wrong. Page intent and customer vocabulary were the more honest inputs.

Structured data, AEO and GEO

Making the product machine readable.

Answer engines and AI search need explicit structure, not inference. I added FAQ sections and JSON-LD.

Honest limits

What this SEO work does not prove

  • No traffic claim. The fixes went live in April 2026. That is not enough time to attribute traffic or ranking movement, so I claim neither.
  • The 403 was a manual test. It showed a real risk to crawl access. It is not evidence of a sustained block.
  • Conformity, not victory. A crawlable site with a clean sitemap and valid schema is the entry ticket. It is not the same as winning a search market.
