Understood · UX/UI and messaging

One product, two very different buyers.

The skill

Turn a product description into a promise a specific person recognises, then clear the path to the action.

The problem

An unclear entry point. No separation between the recruiter journey and the sales journey. Vague calls to action. A long, confusing mobile page.

The proof

The new homepage promise and its call to action are live on worku.tn today.

03 · Mobile

Text before decoration.

The mobile page was long and confusing. I reordered it, lifted the buttons and cut its length.

  1. Text before illustrations. The message arrives before the artwork.
  2. Buttons higher. The action sits where a thumb already is.
  3. Shorter page. Less scrolling to reach the same decision.

04 · Design system

Rules, so pages stop disagreeing.

A small system applied consistently across every page I touched.

ColourA restrained palette. Blue reserved for actions, never decoration.
TypographyA clear hierarchy between headings, subheadings and body text.
ButtonsOne primary action per screen, styled the same way everywhere.
SpacingGenerous whitespace to lower the cognitive load of a B2B page.

Honest limits

What this redesign does not prove

  • No conversion claim. There was no continuous, complete tracking in place during the internship, so I cannot show a conversion lift and do not claim one.
  • No A/B test. These were reasoned design decisions applied to a live product, not a controlled experiment.
  • The message is verifiable, the impact is not. You can load worku.tn and read the new promise. That is the part I can prove.
