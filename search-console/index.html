01 · The instrument

Four reports, four questions.

  • PerformanceAre we found at all, and on what words?
  • URL InspectionCan Google actually fetch this page?
  • SitemapsDoes Google have the map of the site?
  • PagesWhat is indexed, and what is Google refusing?

02 · Interpretation

A good position on almost nothing.

The first read looked like page-one success. Average position %GSC_EARLY_POSITION%, which for a young B2B product in a small market is the kind of number that ends a conversation early.

%GSC_EARLY_IMPRESSIONS%

impressions, across %GSC_EARLY_MONTHS% months. About %GSC_EARLY_PER_DAY% a day. A position is an average over the times you appeared, so ranking seventh out of almost nothing is not the same as ranking seventh.

The second question mattered more than the first: on whose searches?

People who already knew Worku could find it. People with the problem Worku solves could not.

That sentence set the strategy for everything on the SEO page and the redesign: stop optimising for people who type the company name.

03 · Diagnosis

What the tool told me to go and check.

Search Console does not fix anything. It points, and then you go and look.

  • Coverage said pages were missingSo I fetched the site as Googlebot and got a 403 through Cloudflare.
  • Sitemaps had nothing to readSo I checked robots.txt and sitemap.xml directly. Both 404.
  • Page indexing listed old routesSo I traced them to the applicant-tracking product the site used to be.

The fixes, and the evidence for them, are on the SEO page. Repeating them here would pad this one.

The limit of that diagnosis

A manual fetch shows a risk, not a permanent block

  • One request, one moment. The 403 proved Googlebot could be challenged, not that it always was. I treated it as a risk worth removing, and I have never claimed it as a proven sustained block.

The connection I refused to make

%GSC_404S% errors in June ≠ %GSC_404S% redirects in July

The same number turns up in both reports, and it would read well to join them into a before and after. I cannot prove the two sets are the same URLs, so I do not claim it. It is a coincidence until something demonstrates otherwise.

05 · Verification

The new pages exist, and Google found them.

The restructure split one general page into %GSC_AUDIENCE_COUNT% audience pages. This is the check that they did not just ship, but landed.

%GSC_AUDIENCE_TABLE%

%GSC_AUDIENCE_IMPRESSIONS%

impressions on pages that did not exist before, earning %GSC_AUDIENCE_CLICKS% clicks between them. The sitemap was accepted with 14 pages, and URL Inspection returned “URL is on Google” for the pages I checked by hand.

The rule this section exists to enforce

accessible≠indexed≠ranking

Three separate gates. A page can pass the first two and still never be chosen, which is exactly what the next section is about.

06 · Verdict

Found more. Not yet chosen more.

Worked

  • Non-brand went from near-absent to %GSC_NONBRAND_SHARE% of query impressions, across %GSC_NONBRAND_QUERIES% distinct terms.
  • All %GSC_AUDIENCE_COUNT% new audience pages indexed and surfacing.
  • Daily impressions roughly %GSC_IPD_GROWTH% comparing the first month to the last.

Didn’t work yet

  • Non-brand CTR is %GSC_NONBRAND_CTR% against brand’s %GSC_BRAND_CTR%. Visible, not chosen.
  • %GSC_ZERO_QUERY%: %GSC_ZERO_IMPRESSIONS% impressions, position %GSC_ZERO_POSITION%, zero clicks. The clearest example on the property.
  • Total clicks across %GSC_MONTHS% months: %GSC_CLICKS%.

Still unresolved — the ATS ghost

  • The site still ranks position %GSC_ATS_POS_RANGE% for job-seeker queries, with zero clicks on any of them.
  • %GSC_ATS_COUNT% such queries, %GSC_ATS_IMPRESSIONS% impressions, left over from the applicant-tracking era.
  • Legacy positions pull the wrong audience. Unfinished work, not a win.

Ranking first for the wrong person

    %GSC_ATS_LIST%

Job-seeker queries at an average position inside the top three with no clicks. worku.tn, %GSC_PERIOD%. Worku stopped being a job board when the product pivoted to sourcing; Google has not caught up, and a recruiter’s tool is the wrong answer to « %GSC_ATS_EXAMPLE% ».

Visibility widened. Persuasion has not caught up. Titles and descriptions on the non-brand pages are the next job.

Honest limits

What this data does not prove

  • No control group, no A/B test. Growth over the period correlates with the work. Nothing here proves the work caused it.
  • Other things ran in parallel. The rebuild, LinkedIn activity, and ordinary indexing lag after the crawl fixes all moved at the same time. I cannot separate their contributions.
  • No continuous analytics. There is no path from an impression to a sign-up, so I cannot tell you what any of this was worth commercially.
  • Six months is short for search. Positions were still moving at the end of the window, in both directions.
  • Most impressions have no query attached. Google withholds rare queries, so %GSC_QUERY_IMPRESSIONS% of %GSC_IMPRESSIONS% impressions carry a query I can read. The brand and non-brand split is drawn from that visible portion, not the whole.
  • Two figures predate the export. The %GSC_EARLY_POSITION% average position and the June index snapshot come from reads I took at the time, over different windows. They are stored alongside the export rather than recomputed from it.
