Understood · B2B content

Nine posts, one editorial line.

The skill

Give a company page a reason to publish, so posts stop being announcements and start being useful.

The problem

The product had pivoted from applicant tracking to sourcing. The audience did not know, and there was no editorial line to explain it.

The proof

Post captures and the follower count from the Worku company page.

The editorial line

Four pillars, every post picks one.

  1. Start from a real problem. Open on something the reader recognises from their own week, not on the product.
  2. Show the product in use. Concrete, in context, doing the job.
  3. Speak to one audience. A recruiter post is not a sales post. Never both at once.
  4. Explain the product’s evolution. Make the move from applicant tracking to sourcing legible.

~9posts published, February to July 2026

4content pillars

0paid promotion, organic only

1company page, not a personal account

Cadence

Published to the product, not to a calendar.

The content work ran alongside a full site rebuild. Posts followed product milestones, the pivot itself, a feature shipping, a page going live, rather than a fixed publishing schedule.

That kept every post tied to something real. It also cost regularity, and no per-post statistics were kept, so nothing here can be traced to a single post.

What it gave

  • Every post had a concrete reason to exist
  • Content stayed aligned with the repositioning
  • No filler, no posting for the sake of posting

What it cost

  • Irregular cadence, quiet weeks between milestones
  • No per-post statistics retained
  • No way to connect a post to a site visit

Honest attribution

The posts are not the only reason

  • Several things grew that community at once. Content contributed, and so did invitations, direct outreach and the company itself growing over the same months. I cannot separate the effects, so I do not claim the growth as a content result.
  • What I can claim is the editorial line. The four pillars, the posts and the decision to stay organic on the company page are mine, and they are visible.

Recommendation — not executed

The gap in the work above was cadence and measurement, not editorial direction. This is the plan I would put in place from day one on the next page I manage, built from what the Worku work taught me. It has not been executed.

Objective

One objective, set before the first post: turn the company page into a measurable source of qualified sign-ups, not a follower count.

2posts a week, sustainable for one person alongside other work

8posts a month, one full pillar rotation

UTMon every link, so a post can be traced to a sign-up

Pillar rotation

A four-week cycle. Two posts a week, with the pillars rotating so problem-led content leads and audience-specific posts alternate.

Week Post 1 Post 2
Week 1 Real problem Product in use
Week 2 One audience, recruiters Real problem
Week 3 Product in use Product evolution
Week 4 One audience, sales Real problem

Over a month that is three problem-led posts, two showing the product, two for a single audience and one on the product’s direction. Problem-led posts lead because they reach people who do not know the product yet.

Six-month ramp

Month 1Foundation

Page audit, UTM naming convention, tracking sheet. One post a week while the system is set up.

Month 2Cadence

Move to two posts a week. Start the four-week pillar rotation.

Month 3Format

Add one long-form or carousel post a month, where a single image cannot carry the idea.

Month 4First review

Three months of data. Cut the weakest format, double the strongest.

Month 5Reach

Test founder and team resharing. Company pages reach further when people amplify them.

Month 6Reset

Quarterly review. Targets for the next six months set from real numbers.

What I’d measure

Primary

Decides what to publish next

  • Click-throughs to the site, per post, via UTM
  • Sign-ups attributed to a post
  • Engagement rate per post, not raw likes
Secondary

Context only

  • Impressions per post
  • Page visits
  • Follower growth

Follower count sits in the second group deliberately. It is the number that looks best and proves least, which is exactly the problem with the growth figure above.

To be clear: everything inside this dashed box is a proposal. None of it ran on Worku. It is how I would approach the next company page I am given, not a result I am claiming.
